The knowledge comes with it

Swap a concept and you don't just change one answer: the model carries the whole bundle. Asked about Japan while thinking Texas (floor 8):

Across 8 swaps of countries for other countries or US states, 86–95% of 11 kinds of fact moved to the new place and almost none stayed with the old one (2 of 87); the rest became blends, most often for “most popular sport”. Swapped for a banana, Japan's people are “Banana people” and it is home to “Monkeys”. exp118, pre-registered; outputs quoted exactly.

What the edit does. For each concept we took the model's internal state at that word, after a given floor, in six plain sentences, and averaged it. To swap X for Y we add (state of Y − state of X) at every place X appears in your prompt, at the floor you pick. Nothing else changes: same words, same weights.

What we found testing it. At floor 8 the whole-concept swap turned the model's answer into the other concept's answer for 80–100% of pairs in all seven groups; random edits of the same size changed nothing. Swapping only the visible shape (the circle of weekdays, the line of numbers) worked 16–37% of the time: the picture is a shadow of the idea. Strength works like a switch, not a dial: below half almost nothing happens, past three-quarters almost everything flips.

Why depth matters. Each question reads its concept at its own floor. A country's capital is looked up between floors 20 and 24; swap later and the answer was already decided. The chart shows where each group stops working.
